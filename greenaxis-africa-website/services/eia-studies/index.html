Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) Studies in Kenya

Most development projects in Kenya need an environmental impact assessment before work begins. We guide individuals, developers, contractors and businesses through the whole EIA process, from screening to NEMA submission.

What is it?

An Environmental Impact Assessment is a structured study of how a proposed project may affect people, land, water, air and ecosystems, and how those effects will be avoided, reduced or managed. Under the Environmental Management and Co-ordination Act (EMCA) and the EIA regulations, projects that fall within the listed categories must be assessed and licensed by NEMA before implementation. Kenya applies a risk-based approach, so smaller, lower-risk projects typically need a project report, while larger or higher-risk projects need a full study report.

Who needs it?

  • Developers of residential, commercial and mixed-use buildings
  • Contractors starting construction, road, drainage or infrastructure works
  • Businesses setting up or expanding factories, warehouses, farms or workshops
  • Individuals planning a development that may need NEMA approval

What we do

  • Project screening to confirm whether you need a project report or a full study report
  • Site visits, baseline data collection and stakeholder and community consultation
  • Identification of environmental and social impacts and practical mitigation measures
  • Preparation of an Environmental and Social Management Plan (ESMP)
  • Compilation and submission of the report to NEMA
  • Follow-up on comments and support until the licence decision

How it works

  1. Consultation. Tell us about your project, location and goals. We confirm what approvals apply.
  2. Review & Planning. We review your documents, check feasibility and agree scope, timeline and responsibilities.
  3. Assessment & Preparation. We carry out the studies, tests or technical preparation your application needs.
  4. Submission & Follow-up. We submit to NEMA or the county and follow up until a decision.
  5. Approval & Compliance. You receive your approval, and we advise on ongoing compliance.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an EIA before I start building?

If your project falls within the categories listed in the law, you generally need NEMA approval before work begins. We screen your project first and tell you which report applies.

What is the difference between a project report and a study report?

A project report is typically used for lower-risk projects, while a full study report is required for higher-risk or larger projects. The right route depends on the project type, size and location.

Requirements vary by project type, location and county, and regulations change. This page is general information, not legal advice. Contact us to confirm what applies to you.
